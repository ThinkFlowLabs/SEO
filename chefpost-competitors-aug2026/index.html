The competitive picture

The market leader uses the same approach we do

This is the most useful finding in the study, because it tells us the structure we have been building is the right one.

Take a Chef is the largest competitor by a wide margin. They rank for 5,229 searches and hold 306 first place positions. Crucially, they do not win from their homepage. They win with city pages, the same kind of page we have been publishing.

What this confirms

A page about a town of 90,000 people ranks fifth in the country for private chef near me. City pages are not a side project for the market leader, they are the engine. Our library of eighteen city pages and twelve Miami neighbourhood pages is the correct foundation. What those pages have been missing is support: internal links pointing at them, structured data, and clear navigation paths. That is where this month's work went, and where the next phase should go.

There is a second competitor, Hire a Chef, that ranks everything from its homepage with only 427 pages in total. It is worth knowing they exist, but they earn roughly a quarter of what Take a Chef earns, and a homepage cannot rank for Miami, Hoboken and Austin at the same time. Their approach is an interesting outlier, not the model to follow.

The pages are built, the visibility is not

New Jersey

We publish four New Jersey pages: Jersey City, Hoboken, Montclair and Short Hills. Across five New Jersey searches worth $6.74 per click, Take a Chef holds position one and we do not appear in the first thirty results.

This is the clearest illustration of the point above. The pages exist and the content is there. What they lack is the internal linking and authority that would make Google treat them as the answer. It is also the cheapest opportunity in this report, because the expensive part is already done.

Worth borrowing

Four things competitors do well

City pages carry national searches

Covered above, and the single most important structural lesson. Their city pages rank for searches that have no city in them at all.

Two blog posts doing the work of a campaign

One article on what a private chef costs and one on what a private chef earns rank for roughly 17,000 searches a month between them. Two pieces of writing, no ongoing cost.

Chef profiles that rank for the chefs themselves

Searches for individual chefs by name bring them around 5,500 visits a month. Chefpost has chef profiles too, so this is close to free traffic once they are properly visible.

Specific pricing wins AI answers

We ran a real question in ChatGPT about hiring a private chef in Miami. Chefpost was recommended first. A competitor appeared twice, because they publish prices broken down by group size and occasion while we publish one range.

Two decisions worth revisiting

In July we set aside the chef pay and salary searches as the wrong audience, and removed hibachi from our pages. Both look different with this data. Someone searching private chef salary is a chef, and recruiting chefs is half of what a marketplace does. Hibachi carries some of the highest click values in the study.

Beyond Google

AI answers and the local map

AI search

Across ChatGPT answers about private chefs, Take a Chef is cited 121 times and a smaller competitor, Chefry, 61 times. Chefpost appears around 5 times.

The encouraging part is that Chefry has fewer websites linking to it than we do and still gets cited twelve times more often. This is not a question of authority or budget. It is about publishing concrete numbers, which we can act on immediately.

Why we are not in the Miami map results

Our Google listing is in good shape: 76 reviews at a perfect 5.0. The business sitting at the top of the Miami map results has 77 reviews, so reputation is not the gap.

The reason is distance. Google ranks map results by proximity to the searcher, and our verified address is in Aventura. That is a structural constraint, not a reputation problem.

Two openings this creates

First, compete in Aventura, Sunny Isles and Bal Harbour, where we are physically located and already publish pages, and where proximity works in our favour. Second, photographs: we have 30 on the listing against the leader's 112, and that is entirely within our control.

Recommended order

What we would do next

Ordered by return against effort. The first three build on pages that already exist.

  1. Give the city pages the support they need

    New Jersey is the test case: four pages published, five valuable searches, no visibility. Internal links, structured data and breadcrumbs are what turn a published page into a ranking one, and the competitive data shows this is exactly how the market leader wins.

  2. Publish detailed pricing on the Miami page

    Break the price down by group size and occasion rather than a single range. ChatGPT already recommends us first, so this is about occupying space a competitor currently takes. It is an edit to an existing page.

  3. Use the word "hire" across the site

    Nearly 7,000 searches a month where a competitor holds position one and we are absent. Started on Miami, worth extending everywhere.

  4. Build out the cost guide

    Competitor articles earn 17,000 searches a month on this theme, and our cost page is already our strongest performer in AI answers, which suggests the appetite is there.

  5. Add the missing specialities

    Hibachi, Indian, pizza and vegan are roughly 44,000 searches a month between them, all on the same pattern: one page per speciality, the model that already works for sushi.

  6. Compete on the map where we are located

    Aventura, Sunny Isles and Bal Harbour, plus more photographs on the Google listing. No new pages required.

Needs a decision

Three things outside our reach

Each of these is measured and specific, and each needs access or a decision from the Chefpost side.

Bookings are not being recorded

Six of the eight tracking tags point to an analytics account that does not exist, so completed bookings are not counted. We recovered part of it without needing access. Finishing it requires the tag manager. Until then no channel can be credited with a booking, which affects every decision about where to invest.

A content delivery network

First visits remain slow because every photograph is processed by the server on demand. A delivery network removes that work entirely and is the single remaining change that would make a large difference to speed.

A large download on the chef search page

The chef search page downloads 1.8 MB of filter options on every visit, which is most of why it takes over a second to respond. Almost every button on the site leads there.

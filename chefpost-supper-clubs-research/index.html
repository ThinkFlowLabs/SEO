01 · Executive Summary

The answer in 60 seconds.

The instinct behind this brief is correct. The category is rising and unclaimed. But the honest reading of Chefpost's own search data forces one change to the plan, and it is the most important sentence in this report: this category cannot be won in Google right now, because the searches do not exist yet. It can be won in AI assistants, where they already do.

  1. The trend is real and structural, not a spike. US search interest in "supper club" is at its all-time high, up 45% in five years, with peaks every November and December. "Founder dinner" registered its first-ever Google Trends signal in 2026. "Private chef" interest has grown 160% in five years. Diana is early, not late.
  2. Nobody owns it. Across twelve target searches, page one belongs to Reddit threads, Medium posts, Substack newsletters and personal blogs from 2016 to 2018. The biggest competitor, Take a Chef, ranks #1 on every "hire a chef" query using generic city pages and has zero supper-club, founder, corporate or influencer content. A blog that launched in January 2026 already reached #7 on "how to start a supper club". Entry is fast here.
  3. But the demand has not arrived in Google, and we can prove it. Across the last 180 days of Chefpost's Search Console, the entire occasion vocabulary from the brief returned zero impressions: founder dinner, brand dinner, client appreciation dinner, corporate dinner, team dinner, networking dinner, community dinner. Not low. Zero. And the position-one ranking Chefpost holds on "what is a supper club" produced four impressions and no clicks in six months, because an AI Overview answers that question above the results. Ranking first in this category currently pays nothing.
  4. AI search is not a side channel here. It is the channel. The industry estimates of AI question volume are modelled rather than measured, and Section 02 explains exactly how, so we are not resting the case on them. The measured evidence is Chefpost's own: AI traffic went from 0 sessions in May to 126 in August, growing 291% in the last month alone and engaging at 80%, the highest of any channel on the site. On August 25, Perplexity named Chefpost the "best all-around booking platform" and quoted our own price ranges back to us.
  5. We know exactly what earns those citations. GPT cited a small competitor and stated the reason: its page "specifically mentions client appreciation dinners". Exact phrases plus concrete numbers. Chefpost's own 26 recorded AI citations are all cost questions, 18 of them from a single price page. The proof is repeatable: /private-chef-new-jersey was published on August 17 and Perplexity was citing it eight days later, while it still had 7 impressions in Google.

Our recommendation

Build the category, but build it narrow and for the right surface. Three moves: (1) one flagship founder dinners page written for AI citation, with every occasion named verbatim and a price table by headcount, (2) one what is a supper club asset, the only term in the whole cluster with real informational volume at KD 5, and (3) Diana's PR run using the exact same phrases, so interviews and AI answers reinforce each other.

What we are not recommending, and did recommend on August 11: eight pages across the full occasion set. Six of those eight target terms with zero measured demand and no citation path yet. Build one, measure the citations, then decide on the rest.

The founder-dinner search volume is tiny, and that is the point: the vocabulary is forming right now and the pages that define it get quoted when the volume arrives. But judge this work on AI citations and assisted bookings, not on clicks. Measured on clicks it will look like a failure for months, and clicks are the wrong dial.

Confidence: high on the structure, five independent sources agree. The change since August 11 is not new information contradicting old information: it is Chefpost's own Search Console, which we then checked against the category claims.

Question-shaped demand, and how to read this number honestly

What "AI search volume" is, and what it is not. No provider publishes its query logs, so nobody can measure what people actually ask ChatGPT or Perplexity. This metric is modelled: DataForSEO derives it from the questions in Google's "People Also Ask" results, on the reasoning that those are conversational and question-shaped, the same form as a prompt. They describe it as measuring "the popularity of a specific keyword in questions to AI", and call it question-based rather than query-based.

Two consequences worth knowing before quoting the number. Multi-word terms are matched on all words present, not the exact phrase, so "supper club" counts every question containing both "supper" and "club", which sweeps in the Wisconsin-restaurant and definitional mass this report already flags as the wrong intent. Grammatical variants also collapse into a single score. Read 16,411 as a relative index of question-shaped demand, not as 16,411 people asking an assistant how to host a supper club.

The twelve-month series adds the part a single month hides: supper club has been flat between 14,626 and 19,726 all year, so it is not growing in this metric, and "private chef" has fallen from 10,819 to 5,900, down 45% year on year. The hard evidence that this channel is real for Chefpost is not this number. It is the 126 AI-assistant sessions in GA4 and the citations in Section 06.

03 · The Reality Check

Position one,
and it changes nothing.

Search Console, last 180 days. A Chefpost blog post from April 2024 does rank first for the questions that define this category. We then checked what that ranking actually delivered, and the answer reframes the entire opportunity.

· · ·
https://chefpost.com/blog/the-benefits-of-hosting-regular-in-person-events
Published Apr 2024 · H1: "From Dinner Parties to Supper Club: A New Way to Grow Your Community"
RANKS #1 · EARNS NOTHING
Where it ranks
Position 1.0: "what is a supper club", "what's the difference between a supper club and a restaurant", "how to start a supper club with friends". Position 2.3: "supper club".
This part is real. Google does trust Chefpost on this topic, and the head query carries 5,400 searches a month at KD 5.
What that ranking actually delivered
Over 180 days: "what is a supper club" produced 4 impressions. "Supper club" produced 3. The whole page, across all 18 queries it touches, produced 38 impressions and 0 clicks, and 11 of those impressions were our own site: checks.
A #1 ranking on a 5,400/mo term returning 4 impressions in six months is not an optimization problem. It means Google is not sending that query to the results at all.
Why
An AI Overview answers "what is a supper club" above the organic results. It is a definitional question, which is exactly the kind Google now answers itself.
We have seen this on this domain before. The anti-inflammatory post ranks in the top 15 on a 22,000-impression term and converts almost none of it, for the same structural reason. Optimizing the title will not change the mechanism.
The correction, stated plainly. On August 11 we called this post a beachhead and recommended building a full pillar on top of it. Checking it against 180 days of Search Console changes that: the ranking is real and the traffic behind it is not there. The term is still worth one page, because it is the only informational term in the cluster with genuine volume and a KD of 5. It is not worth a pillar plus four spokes.
And next door, the whitespace is total in both directions. Over 180 days, queries containing founder dinner, brand dinner, client appreciation dinner, corporate dinner, team dinner, networking dinner or community dinner generated zero Chefpost impressions between them. Nothing to cannibalize, but also nothing arriving. This vocabulary has no Google demand today.
The one adjacent signal that is real. "Where can I book a private chef for a dinner party" sits at position 1.5 and 2.8 today. Long, conversational, question-shaped queries are the ones Chefpost already wins, and they are also the shape of what people type into AI assistants. That is the thread this report follows from here. Meanwhile /events ranks #67 for "chef for a dinner party", a $14.28 CPC query, on a 255-word page with no H1.

AUGUST 25 · THE CHANNEL IS NO LONGER THEORETICAL

Chefpost's AI-assistant traffic, measured in GA4: 0 sessions in May, 7 in June, 45 in July, 126 in August. Up 291% in the last month, with an 80% engagement rate, the highest of any channel on the site, ahead of direct, organic and social. Of 137 sessions in the last 28 days, 131 came from ChatGPT.

On the same day, asked which service to use for a private chef doing weekly meal prep, Perplexity ranked Chefpost first, calling it the "best all-around booking platform", and cited /private-chef-miami and /meal-prep while quoting our own published price range back at us. Asked how to hire a private chef in New Jersey, it cited /private-chef-new-jersey and quoted its pricing.

That New Jersey page was published on August 17. Perplexity was citing it eight days later, at a point when it had earned exactly 7 impressions in Google. A page can be worth something in this channel long before Google notices it exists. That is the single most useful finding in this report.

THE OTHER HALF: CHATGPT STILL DOES NOT SEE US LOCALLY

The same two questions asked of ChatGPT with web search returned no Chefpost citation at all. It answered with Google Business Profile listings instead: Chef Collin (4.7 stars, 31 reviews), VJ Cuisine (5.0, 69 reviews), Bespoke Bites (4.9, 27). The split is diagnostic rather than random: Perplexity reads the web index and Chefpost wins; ChatGPT answers local questions from the map pack, where Chefpost barely exists. Since ChatGPT is 96% of this channel, the Google Business Profile is the constraint on the fastest-growing traffic source on the site. That is a separate workstream from this brief, and it is now the higher-value one.

Data sources and method notes
  • Search volumes and CPC: Google Ads live data, US, August 11 2026. Keyword difficulty and intent: DataForSEO Labs. Unscored KD reported as a dash.
  • Trends: Google Trends US, five-year window, shared index scale; yearly averages computed from weekly data.
  • SERPs: DataForSEO live organic, depth 10 to 20, US and Miami geolocation, August 11.
  • AI answers: DataForSEO LLM endpoint, live, web search enabled, run August 11 and re-run August 25. Citation-field data: LLM mentions database, ChatGPT platform, US.
  • AI search volumes are modelled, not measured. No AI provider publishes query logs. DataForSEO states these values are "calculated using statistical data from questions in the 'People Also Ask' SERP element" via a proprietary algorithm, and describe the result as question-based rather than query-based. Multi-word terms match on all words present rather than the exact phrase, and grammatical variants are collapsed. Figures are the July 2026 month with the twelve-month series checked alongside. We report them as a relative index only, and rest the argument on Chefpost's own GA4 and citation data instead.
  • Chefpost first-party data: Search Console API (sc-domain property), 180-day and 28-day windows through August 23 2026, the freshest day available. GA4 API, property 255030659, channel and landing-page reports for the same windows.
  • Known caveats: "press dinner" volume (590) is a news artifact of the April correspondents' dinner and was excluded. "Brand dinner ideas" is a recipe-intent SERP; the marketing intent lives in "brand activation dinner" phrasing. DataForSEO's ranked-keyword index lags live SERPs by 2 to 6 weeks, so Search Console is treated as ground truth throughout. GA4 "AI Assistant" is Google's own channel grouping and undercounts assistants that send no referrer, so the 126-session figure is a floor rather than a ceiling.

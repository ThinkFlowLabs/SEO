Shipped this period

Three changes are live on the site

All three are in production and verified. Each one closed a gap we could measure rather than one we assumed.

New Jersey now has a page

Searches for a private chef in New Jersey run about 2,950 a month, and the site had no page for them. Both likely addresses returned a "page not found" error. The four New Jersey town pages were left competing for statewide searches on their own.

There is now a New Jersey page that gathers all four towns, and the short form of the address redirects to it instead of failing.

The town pages were missing "NJ"

Hoboken, Jersey City, Montclair and Short Hills each used the words "New Jersey" six to eight times, and the abbreviation "NJ" zero times. Anyone searching with the abbreviation, which is how most people type it, could not find them.

All four now carry it in the page title and description.

The meal prep page was pointing at itself incorrectly

The page carried 2,516 impressions but sat at an average position of 23.6, which is page three. It also told Google its official address was a slightly different one that immediately redirects back, and it carried none of the structured information Google uses to understand a service page.

Both are fixed, and the page now speaks to the searches that indicate someone wants to hire.

The investigation

Our largest source of impressions cannot be converted

One article on the site collects more search impressions than almost anything else. We had it marked as the top priority to fix. Looking at it properly changed that conclusion, and we would rather tell you now than spend the budget finding out.

The obvious explanation is that the entry looked unappealing in the results, and that a better title would fix it. That was our assumption too. It is not what is happening.

On a dozen variations of this topic the site is already inside the top five results, which is normally the most valuable position in search. The clicks are still zero.

When a page sits in fifth place and earns no clicks at all, the entry is not the problem. Something is taking the clicks before anyone reaches the results.

Google answers this question itself, above the results

We pulled the live Google results page for the main search. Sitting in fourth place is Google's own AI summary, and inside it is a complete three-day meal plan with breakfast, lunch and dinner written out. The person searching has their answer without visiting any website.

Below that, the actual results are the University of Arizona's integrative medicine centre, Healthline, a registered dietitian, and Harvard Health. Google's summary draws on Harvard, Johns Hopkins, the Cleveland Clinic and the US Department of Veterans Affairs.

What this means This is a health question, and Google answers those with medical institutions. A private chef marketplace is not going to be preferred over Harvard for dietary advice, and no rewrite changes that. The article also does not contain the seven-day plan its title promises, so improving the title alone would have made a promise the page cannot keep.

We checked a second article with the same pattern, the healthy meal prep ideas piece, which holds 4,167 impressions at position 5.2 with one click. Its results page is led by a recipe carousel, then recipe blogs, Pinterest, Instagram and YouTube. It is a recipe request, not a request to hire anyone. The conclusion is the same.

Three clusters inside that pool are ready to move

What a chef costs

340 impressions, zero clicks, sitting between positions 8.7 and 18.3 across six variations of "how much does a private chef cost".

We already have a page built for exactly this question. It is close to the first page and not quite there, which makes it the cheapest improvement available.

Chefs searched by name

305 impressions for individual chefs, at positions between 5.1 and 10.1. People are searching for Chef Gina, Chef Farid, Chef Carolina and Chef Mehmet by name.

That is the highest intent traffic on the site and nobody has optimised the chef profile pages. This is unclaimed.

Miami, still on page two

434 impressions across "private chefs miami", "best private chef miami" and similar, at positions 11 to 20.

The Miami page was rebuilt recently, so some movement is expected. Worth measuring before doing more.

Recommended order

What we suggest doing next

Ordered by return against effort, based on what the measurements above show rather than on general practice.

  1. Improve the private chef cost page Ready now

    Six searches about pricing, 340 impressions, no clicks, all sitting just off the first page. The page exists and needs sharpening rather than building. This is the shortest distance between effort and result on the whole site.

  2. Claim the chef name searches Ready now

    People are already searching for individual chefs by name and finding us in the top ten. The chef profile pages have never been reviewed for search. Improving their titles, descriptions and structured information would convert an existing position into visits, and these are the most motivated visitors on the site.

  3. Finish the older pages Planned

    Fifteen pages predate the current build. Six have no structured information at all, eleven show no navigation trail, ten have titles too long to display fully, and one has no main heading. None of this is urgent, and all of it is straightforward.

  4. Build the dietary meal prep landing Planned

    The commercial half of the meal prep market is roughly 2,180 searches a month with low competition and strong growth. The section now on the meal prep page is the first step. A dedicated page is the second, once we see how the first performs.

  5. Confirm the Miami consolidation held Planned

    In mid September it will be six weeks since we pointed the old Miami article at the main Miami page. If Google has combined them, nothing more is needed. If it has not, we redirect the article permanently. This is a scheduled check, not a task.

Needs a decision

Three items outside our reach

Each of these limits results and none can be resolved without access or a decision from your side.

A content delivery network

First visits are still slow because every photograph is processed by the server on demand, and there is no delivery network in front of the site. We confirmed this again this period.

It would also solve a second problem. During each deployment there is a short window where a visitor can load the site without its styling, because the page and its design files briefly come from different versions. A delivery network removes both issues at once.

Booking measurement

Six of the eight tracking tags still point to an analytics account that does not exist, so completed bookings are not counted. Until the tag manager is available, no channel can be credited with a booking, which affects every decision about where to invest.

The chef search page download

The chef search page downloads 1.8 MB of filter options on every visit, which is most of why it takes over a second to respond. Almost every button on the site leads there, so this affects the experience broadly.

Worth knowing The staging environment runs in development mode. That makes it several times slower than the live site by design. It does not affect visitors, but any impression of the site's speed formed there would be misleading. Speed should only be judged on the live site.

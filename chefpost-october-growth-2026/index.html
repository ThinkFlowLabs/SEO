01 / The decision

Improve the pages already attracting demand.

Our first sprint should combine pricing and contact-path corrections, Miami query ownership, and mobile performance. These affect the same customer journey: a person discovers Chefpost, evaluates a service, finds a suitable chef and contacts the team.

Build on this

The foundation is substantially cleaner.

All 551 current sitemap URLs returned direct HTTP 200. That set has no duplicate titles or descriptions and no missing titles, H1s, descriptions or canonicals. Nine priority URLs inspected in Search Console were indexed with matching canonicals.

Focus here

Discovery still loses momentum.

Mobile loading remains slow. Prices and inclusions conflict across some pages. The chef filter needs JavaScript to expose its roster. Miami transactional keywords are unstable even while overall visibility improves.

What success means

More received, qualified inquiries from the markets Chefpost can serve.

Clicks, rankings and citations are leading indicators. An accepted form event does not prove the team received it; a phone click does not prove a connected call. Confirm contact receipt and qualification before treating these as business results.

1. Rework the cost guide around verifiable answers.

The cost guide has 18,289 impressions and 54 clicks, a 0.295% CTR. For this page, the exact query “how much does a private chef cost” had 877 impressions and zero clicks at average position 7.82. Review its real search snippets and competing answers, then test a clearer title and opening answer. Explain session versus per-person pricing, guest minimums, grocery inclusions, staffing and additional charges with current linked offers.

Use a compact price comparison with an inquiry link beside it. Do not force every service into one “all included” claim. Review query-level clicks and CTR after an equivalent period, with device and position context. This is a testable opportunity, not a forecast of guaranteed extra traffic.

Miami rose from nine to 23 clicks, but its blended average position of 16.89 hides the head term: “private chef miami” averaged 26.65 with 88 impressions and no clicks for this page in GSC.

Between September 15 and October 12, that exact query moved from position 26 with the old blog URL to position 74 with the homepage. “Personal chefs in Miami” moved from 20 with the blog to 63 with the homepage. 3. Strengthen service pages before commissioning a large article backlog.

Meal Prep, Thanksgiving, Sushi, Postpartum and Long Island already bring search visitors. Give them specific chef and offer links, a dependable inquiry path and evidence supporting their claims. The native article group generated only five clicks across 1,374 page-level impressions in this comparison; the newsletter group generated one click. Continue the native-blog plus Substack distribution model, while directing editorial effort toward proven service needs.

Authority needs relevant relationships.

Semrush estimates 683 US ranking keywords and 749 monthly organic visits, compared with September's 671 and 594. These estimates are separate from GSC/GA4. Its backlink database also jumped from 403 to 1,553 referring domains, yet Authority Score remained 18. Of 30 recently detected referring domains sampled, 28 were generic backlink/checker sites with score two; the other two scored four.

That sample does not establish useful authority growth or a penalty. Prioritize real chef bios linking to Chefpost, event-planner partnerships, local hospitality referrals and useful local editorial coverage. Do not buy link packages or automatically disavow domains based on low scores.

The Thanksgiving prompt cited Chefpost once and omitted it on the repeat. Weekly meal prep did the reverse. Postpartum retrieved a Chefpost page but did not recommend it in the final answer. Retrieval, a brand mention and a clickable citation are different outcomes. October 1 tests used gpt-5-6, a different model, so changes between panels cannot be attributed to website work.

Separately, a Google AI Overview for a Miami meal-prep query mentioned Chefpost in Aventura with its rating and review count, using a Google source rather than a chefpost.com citation. This is encouraging entity recognition, not a direct website citation or proof of comprehensive AI coverage.

Make every cited answer dependable.

  • One source for offers: maintain explicit price unit, grocery inclusion, guest minimum, location, chef identity, current menu and verified availability. Render those facts in visible HTML and matching structured data.
  • Make decisions easy: add concise, factual answers to cost, service and city pages, with named chefs and direct menu links. Show when information was substantively reviewed and who supplied operational facts.
  • Supply evidence: publish authentic chef experience, real reviews and documented local examples. Label illustrative generated images so they cannot be mistaken for proof of completed client events.
  • Keep crawlers eligible: preserve public-page discovery while controlling search, training and user-requested bots separately. Verify actual fetches and WAF behavior; a robots allowance alone is insufficient.
  • Measure repeatability and usefulness: rerun the same prompts and settings, language and location, recording the returned model, sources, price accuracy and variability. Compare matching models where possible; this provider does not expose model selection. Add Claude testing before making cross-assistant claims.

Google states that its AI search features use the same fundamental SEO practices; there is no special AI schema or required AI file. Keep pages indexed, eligible for snippets, useful and accurately structured. A public read-only discovery API can follow once offer and availability data are dependable. A chatbot or MCP booking integration is not the first sprint. Google guidance · OpenAI crawler documentation · Anthropic crawler documentation.

Mobile performance needs a controlled engineering pass.

Fresh complete Lighthouse mobile reports measured 6.38 seconds LCP on the homepage and 9.04 seconds on Meal Prep, with performance scores 40 and 29 respectively. Total blocking time exceeded 2.6 seconds on both. Each is one simulated run, not a field Core Web Vitals result. PageSpeed field retrieval returned an error, so no current field pass can be claimed.

The reports identify roughly 1.25 MB and 1.12 MB of unused JavaScript. About 0.89 MB per page came from googletagmanager.com, including multiple configuration variants and a legacy measurement ID. This is network evidence, not proof of duplicate recorded events. Audit the tag inventory with the GTM owner, remove only confirmed obsolete work, defer nonessential vendors, narrow initial Maps loading and preserve accessibility and consent behavior. Recheck contact and analytics flows afterward.

Availability exists, but crawlers receive a weaker version.

Thirty-six of 48 profile HTMLs exposed a positive next date, while all 423 sitemap product pages emitted “No Suggested dates available.” In the browser, Abel's profile and $450-per-session meal-prep offer both showed a date, and requesting times returned selectable slots. The problem is inconsistent initial HTML, not demonstrated absence of inventory. Server-render an accurate availability summary or explicitly state that dates must be checked; never use a false “none available” default.

Correct content that can mislead customers and assistants.

Thanksgiving and Christmas say groceries cost extra, while the cost guide presents different inclusion rules. Some holiday coverage claims also conflict with Boston's opening-market status. Resolve those facts from actual offers and operations. Route “Talk to our concierge” to contact; keep “Browse chefs” for marketplace discovery.

Older articles still include a Christmas 2022 title, a New Year's welcome to 2023, a $28-per-person meal claim and unsupported health wording such as “heal your gut.” Review those articles substantively, with appropriately qualified review for health claims. The design rollout preserved the original copy; attractive formatting did not validate those statements. The World Cup 2026 page also needs a clear past-event treatment and an appropriate future discovery path.

Broken-link reconciliation

Some links are still broken, despite the clean main sitemap.

Semrush’s older 663-page crawl reported 46 broken-link occurrences. All 36 historical 5xx targets now return HTTP 200. This observation does not establish the original cause or prove that intermittent server errors are permanently resolved. Two native articles still contain six links to five dead booking.chefpost.com destinations. Two live Substack posts contain five links to four retired offers that return HTTP 404. Fix those source links and map renamed offers only where a genuine successor exists. A dead DNS record is not a 301 redirect.

01 / La decisión

Base para avanzar

Más consultas recibidas y calificadas desde los mercados que Chefpost puede atender.

1. Reorganizar la guía de costos alrededor de respuestas verificables.

La guía de costos tiene 18,289 impresiones y 54 clics, con un CTR del 0.295%. Para esta página, la consulta exacta “how much does a private chef cost” tuvo 877 impresiones y cero clics, con una posición media de 7.82. Hay que revisar cómo aparece realmente en los resultados y qué respuestas ofrecen los competidores, y después probar un título y una respuesta inicial más claros. Debe explicar el precio por sesión frente al precio por persona, los mínimos de invitados, los ingredientes incluidos, el personal y los cargos adicionales, con enlaces a ofertas actuales.

Conviene presentar una comparación breve de precios con un enlace de consulta al lado. No todos los servicios deben quedar bajo una misma promesa de “todo incluido”. Después de un período equivalente, revisar clics y CTR por consulta, considerando dispositivo y posición. Es una oportunidad que se puede poner a prueba, no una previsión de tráfico adicional garantizado.

Miami pasó de nueve a 23 clics, pero su posición media combinada de 16.89 oculta la consulta principal: “private chef miami” promedió 26.65, con 88 impresiones y ningún clic para esta página en GSC.

Hay que volver a comprobar la redirección del antiguo artículo de Miami, la coherencia de los canonicals y los textos de los enlaces internos.
